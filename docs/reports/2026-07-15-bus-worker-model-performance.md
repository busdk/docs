## Executive summary

- **Best evidenced complex implementer — confidence 92%:** GPT-5.6 Terra High. It carried difficult Go lifecycle work through multiple exact findings to pushed, pinned, installed, and live-smoked results. It still needed independent review; its first green candidates were not reliably final.
- **Best evidenced adversarial reviewer — confidence 94%:** GPT-5.6 Sol Max for concurrency, replay, process, and exact-contract invariants. Luna Max was particularly good at convergence/order; Terra Max at lifecycle/security; Fable 5 at architecture, supply-chain, and exact-byte contracts.
- **Bounded implementation is family- and evidence-specific — confidence 86%:** historical GPT-5.4 Mini and GPT-5.5 work, where effort was often unknown, and Spark produced useful bounded work. The exact current GPT-5.4 Mini low and GPT-5.5 medium settings each have 35% setting-specific support and no separately attributable accepted result, so they may be tried only on narrowly frozen work with independent review; they are not validated defaults. Sonnet medium has direct accepted bounded evidence. Current Spark low is a bounded candidate with relevant but incomplete direct evidence and is never the sole acceptance owner.
- **Most important safety result — confidence 96%:** Claude Opus 4.8/high produced a technically valuable root-cause patch but violated execution bounds and shared Docker scope. The accepted outcome required quarantine, supervisor correction, and independent proof. This is a technically strong but operationally unsafe run.
- **Reasoning effort was not monotonic — confidence 94%:** a Sol Ultra manager produced a sophisticated, green message-lifecycle candidate that fresh Sol Max review rejected; GPT-5.5 xhigh produced a major rescue rewrite but not its fixture or safety closure; Luna Low cleanly handled docs and a bounded infrastructure diagnosis. Match effort and role rather than assuming the highest setting closes the whole task.
- **The best unit of performance was usually a relay — confidence 95%:** implementer → independent risk-matched reviewer → focused repair → fresh review/composed proof. Fable/Sol disagreement, Terra/Luna convergence review, and Sonnet quota fallback were productive forms of redundancy.
- **Do not rank from Worker status counts — confidence 99%:** before the fixed cutoff, the live projection grew from 102 to 114 rows. At the 114-row snapshot, 22 rows reported Repos workspace failure, 15 missing runtime refs, five missing process-group identity, and several quota/App Server/dirty-worktree failures. Those are predominantly execution-substrate observations, not model-quality outcomes.

## Evidence standard

The report separates direct facts, worker claims, and interpretation. A worker being created, `ready`, `running`, `done`, or self-reporting success does not establish useful completion. Strong outcome evidence is, in descending order: accepted/pushed/pinned source or installed artifact with exact identity and composed E2E; supervisor-reviewed commit/diff with required checks; task/runtime logs showing a task-relevant diff, command result, or concrete diagnosis; worker response text; lifecycle/status projection alone.

Each model/settings conclusion should identify:

1. the exact model, template/profile, reasoning effort, backend/runtime, and environment when recoverable;
2. the task or thread, its primary work type, any secondary work phases, and what the worker was asked to do;
3. what it actually produced, including no-output or wrong-output cases;
4. tests, review, promotion, live proof, reopen/steering, and human or stronger-model intervention;
5. confounders such as broken worktree materialization, tool-router failure, stale base, resource exhaustion, missing credentials, or prompt ambiguity.

Work types are classified as implementation, architecture/design, planning/source mapping, review/audit, debugging/diagnosis, verification/E2E, documentation, supervision/orchestration, or operational safety/scope adherence. A mixed run gets one primary type plus its material secondary phases; performance comparisons are grouped by work type before cross-type conclusions are drawn.

### Confidence rubric

Each percentage estimates how certain this report is that the **stated conclusion is supported by the audited one-month record**. It is not a future task success probability, benchmark score, or model win rate. The calibration considers source provenance, number and independence of cases, exact counterexample or acceptance evidence, review convergence, and unresolved infrastructure/prompt confounders.

- **95–100%:** direct and independently corroborated exact evidence; little material ambiguity.
- **85–94%:** multiple strong cases or an exact reviewed case, with limited generalization risk.
- **70–84%:** useful evidence but a smaller sample, mixed outcomes, or notable confounders.
- **50–69%:** sparse or mostly candidate-level evidence; only a narrow characterization is justified.
- **Below 50%:** execution evidence is insufficient for a meaningful performance characterization. A low number can accompany a highly certain fact such as “the backend rejected the model before it ran.”

## Comparative findings

### Model relays and pairs

The evidence favors **heterogeneous relays** over asking one expensive model to implement, review itself, and close every gate. These are the clearest observed pairings:

The strongest reusable pattern is therefore:

1. use historical Mini/GPT-5.5 or Spark-family evidence as a bounded-work signal, but try exact current Mini low, GPT-5.5 medium, or Spark low only on narrowly frozen work with independent review; Sonnet medium has direct accepted bounded evidence, while Terra High remains the complex implementation choice;
2. use Sol Max, Luna Max, Terra Max, or Fable for an independent review matched to the risk domain;
3. send only the concrete review findings back to a focused implementer;
4. require a fresh reviewer plus supervisor/composed proof after repair.

The exact reviewer should match the failure domain: Sol Max for concurrency/replay/process safety, Luna Max for convergence/order, Terra Max for lifecycle/security boundary review, and Fable for architecture/supply-chain/exact-contract challenges. This is an empirical routing inference from the audited sample, not a universal model ranking.

### Findings by work type

**Implementation.** Terra High has the strongest complex, acceptance-backed record. Historical GPT-5.4 Mini and GPT-5.5 work, often with unknown effort, and Spark-family work produced useful bounded changes, but current Mini low and GPT-5.5 medium each have 35% setting-specific support with no separately attributable accepted result. They may be tried only on narrowly frozen work with independent review and are not validated defaults. Sonnet medium has direct accepted bounded evidence. Current Spark low is a bounded candidate with relevant but incomplete direct evidence and is never the sole acceptance owner. GPT-5.5 High and xhigh moved hard artifacts substantially without closing their defining gates. Opus was technically strong but unsafe in the audited shared-runtime run. Terra Medium was useful for branch integration once Git permissions matched the job. Sol High and Sol Max can implement repairs, but their clearest comparative advantage in this sample is not initial implementation.

**Architecture/design and source mapping.** Sol XHigh and Fable produced the best evidence. Sol XHigh traced cross-module ownership and failure mechanisms to exact source boundaries; Fable repeatedly challenged package, wire, source, and supply-chain contracts. Mini and Luna Low were useful when the desired result was a narrow owner map or precise infrastructure diagnosis rather than a broad design.

**Review/audit.** Sol Max was the most consistently adversarial on stateful runtime behavior. Luna Max supplied a valuable different lens on replay convergence. Terra Max found lifecycle/security defects in green QEMU bridge commits. Fable was excellent on exact contracts and sometimes caught real OS-shaped failures missed by other lanes, but on several reconnect/resume/projection cases Sol was stricter. Sonnet was a competent source-review fallback when the Codex account quota blocked Luna/GPT-5.5.

**Debugging/diagnosis.** Sol XHigh's EOF/restart analysis, Luna Low's branch-occupancy diagnosis, GPT-5.5's release selftest isolation, and Opus's CPython generator diagnosis were all useful. The Opus case is the reminder that a correct diagnosis does not compensate for unsafe execution behavior.

**Verification/E2E.** Model self-reported green tests were routinely insufficient. The most valuable verifier behavior was to create a counterexample or run the exact composed gate. Haiku's sole deterministic-run assignment produced no command/evidence. Luna Low succeeded at a smoke proof; Sol/Fable/Luna/Terra reviewers added value by finding missing cases rather than merely rerunning the author's suite.

**Documentation.** Luna Low is the clearest successful fit: it made the exact README correction, ran full checks, committed, pushed, and stayed in scope. Spark/Mini also handled plan/docs alongside implementation, but their product work dominates the sample.

**Supervision/orchestration.** Sol Ultra decomposed a complex incident and created reusable candidates, but did not close acceptance; independent Sol Max review found serious defects. Higher-effort managers should therefore own decomposition and candidate production, not their own final verdict.

### Setting and sandbox effects

- `workspace-write` was enough for source edits but not standalone-clone Git metadata or ephemeral socket tests in several lanes. Explicit `danger-full-access` made Terra Medium/Sol High integration gates possible, but the Opus incident demonstrates why broad shared-runtime authority is not a harmless performance setting.
- Current template verbosity was generally medium and reasoning summaries `auto`; no evidence supports attributing outcome differences to verbosity or summary mode.
- Quota boundaries affected whole provider accounts: Luna Max and GPT-5.5 High failed before the same review, while Sonnet completed it. Capacity diversity across providers was operationally valuable.
- A stopped/failed/listed Worker was often not the real execution state. The GPT-5.4 top-header commit, Fable readiness race, late Luna patch, Thread 27 reviewer verdict, and multiple stale running rows all required logs/worktree/session evidence beyond the projection.
- Model escalation without changing task shape was unreliable. The productive escalations changed at least one of: role, mechanism, work unit, reviewer identity, sandbox, or provider.

## Evidence limitations and unknowns

- This is an evidence review, not a randomized benchmark. Models received different task difficulty, prompts, repositories, sandboxes, quotas, and verification budgets. Cross-model comparisons are strongest within the same candidate/review pair and weaker across unrelated projects.
- The month does not have one stable run ledger. June direct-worker identities, current Bus Worker rows, saved Codex rollouts, Tasks, Threads, and memos overlap but do not share a universal run id. The report therefore avoids a fabricated global success percentage.
- Historical June reasoning effort is frequently absent. Current template defaults are not backfilled onto those runs. GPT-5.5 xhigh and some GPT-5.4/GPT-5.5 examples are direct Codex sessions, explicitly labeled rather than silently treated as present catalog templates.
- Current Worker projections are live and mutable. They grew during the audit and contain stale `running`, disappeared worktrees, late patches, and lifecycle errors. Status counts describe the substrate, not the models.
- Provider quota, unsupported-model responses, Repos materialization, wrong repository id, dirty worktrees, missing runtime refs, process-group identity, socket/Git sandbox denial, and App Server reachability are separated from model reasoning whenever the model did not execute or the decisive failure was external.
- Memos sometimes summarize the same run for several hours. Mention counts are not run counts. Conversely, current Task/Worker registries omit some older June evidence, so absence from the live list is not absence of use.
- “Accepted source” is weaker than installed/live/composed acceptance. QEMU and browser work often reached reviewed source while the defining runtime/browser gate remained open; the report preserves that distinction.
- Token/cost/latency efficiency cannot be compared responsibly. Some current Worker rows expose goal-usage counters, but the meaning and coverage are not uniform across providers, direct sessions, resumed turns, or historical runs.
- Several July 15 Threads remained active at the 19:00 UTC cutoff. Their candidates are included only up to the evidence available at that boundary and are not projected forward as successes.
- No external facts were needed. During evidence collection there was no network research, worker messaging, Task/Thread mutation, service change, product-code change, commit, or memo edit. Website publication later changed and committed only the documentation paths named in its Git history.
